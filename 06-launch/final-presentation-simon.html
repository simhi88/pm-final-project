05Strategy

Problem hook

We must solve the rising cost by addressing the user’s uncertainties & possibly panic situation.

Value proposition

For our consumers, we will offer a guided portal journey because we are losing most starters today right at the beginning of user journey, to align our product priorities with our north-star strategic goal of 80% digital self-service Europewide.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on the brief’s description of Mara stalling at the income step and calling an agent, and on 31% of journey starters contacting an agent within 7 days, I believe that a guided portal journey explaining why income is requested, what is affordable and what a missed payment means, for variable-income consumers with multiple claims like Mara, will result in more of them agreeing a plan without calling an agent, as measured by a 5-point reduction (31% to 26%) in 7-day agent contact among journey starters. I will protect the 60-day missed-payment rate on new agreements (19% baseline, must not rise in any tracked subgroup). I will make a go/no-go decision after 10 weeks of pilot data in Germany, which allows 60 days of post-agreement follow-up.

PRD highlights: “Why we ask”

At every income question, Mara sees in one plain sentence why we ask, so she keeps going instead of calling an agent.

Must have

  • An inline note on every income-step question, including the document request
  • A reason of 20 words or fewer; “how it’s used” only if Ops confirms it
  • Always visible on a 360px phone, no tap needed

Won’t have, now

  • AI-generated explanations, chatbot, or any change to the income step itself

Evals

  • At least 80% comprehension accuracy
  • Time on task at most 110% of control
  • 100% of notes pass the copy checklist

09Launch

GTM Launch Plan: “Why we ask”

Goal

Engagement. Encourage the users to add the information we ask for.

Audience

Mara, 38, a shift worker with variable but stable income and two open claims. She can’t pay the balance in one go and opens the portal on her phone, after a digital reminder, with very little time. Pilot segment: Germany.

Launch tier

SMinimal. We’re only adjusting content in an already existing feature.

Channels

Owned: Consumer Portal Owned: E-Mail with deeplink to the portal Owned: Letter

≥ 81% floor

Equivalent to a miss rate of 19% or less.

Warning signThe miss rate rises above 19%.

Bad signal to watch for: Many people see the instalment, and the call rate stays at 31%. Or calls fall and the 60-day miss rate rises. Then she agreed without being able to keep the instalment.

Phase 1, beta

50/50 in the one segment, 14 days of enrollment plus 7 days of follow-up. Do not ship on day 21.

Phase 2, launch

Only once, 60 days after agreement, the keep rate in the variant is at least 81% and the call rate has fallen by at least 5 percentage points below 31%. Portal only, for this segment.

Phase 3, after launch

Keep reading the keep rate, with the one-pager in use.

Likely post-launch decision: Repeat. Double only if both are true: at least 5 percentage points fewer calls and a keep rate of at least 81%.

10Story

Friction points and the aha moment

Friction points

She doesn’t know why the information is neededFixed by “Why we ask”
She fears a wrong answer will backfirePartly fixed
She can’t find the documentsNot fixed
Her shift-based income fits no single figureNot fixed
She doesn’t know if a plan is affordable or what a missed payment meansNot fixed

The “aha” moment

The aha moment is the moment Mara realises the income question is not a test.

It happens at Q2, the typical-month question, because her variable income makes it the hardest to answer. She reads “We ask so we can suggest payments that fit a normal month for you. A close estimate is fine.” Within seconds she stops treating the figure as a trap, types an approximate number, and continues without calling an agent.

Key takeaways

  • “Why we ask” fixes half of the moment of misery. It answers “why is this needed?” It does not answer “where do I find a document?” or “my income fits no payslip.” Document guidance and variable-income input are what the 31% to 26% target also depends on.
  • Variable-income input is the biggest gap in the roadmap. I scored it Value 5, and it isn’t scheduled. Without it, Q2 still asks Mara for one typical figure.
  • The critical path is approval, not engineering. The build is copy plus components. Legal and Ops sign-off on every sentence sets the release date.
  • The “how it’s used” line carries the most value and the most risk. It addresses her fear that a wrong answer will backfire. But “a close estimate is fine” can invite rough figures and put the 19% guardrail at risk, so it ships only if Ops confirms it’s true today.
  • Neutral wording matters. Any hint that a higher or lower figure helps her changes behaviour, so the copy can’t suggest it.
  • You can’t prove the effect yet. Income-step drop-off has no baseline, so the instrumentation dashboard has to ship first, with a holdout to separate this feature from the other Now items.
  • Five test participants give a direction, not proof. The comprehension and time-on-task evals tell you whether to keep going, not whether you’ve hit the target.

What I would do next

  1. Ship instrumentation first, with a holdout, to get the baseline.
  2. Get Legal and Ops sign-off on every sentence.
  3. Schedule document guidance and variable-income input, which the 31% to 26% target also depends on.
